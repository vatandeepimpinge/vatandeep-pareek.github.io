Best Semantic Caching Solutions for a Governed LLM Gateway in 2026

Explore how semantic caching fits into enterprise LLM gateways, with a focus on governance, security, cost control, and observability.

Caching is easy to add and hard to govern. A standalone semantic cache will happily shave tokens off your bill, but once it sits in front of production traffic it inherits every hard question your platform already faces: who is allowed to hit it, whose budget a served response counts against, whether a cached answer leaks sensitive data, and how you prove any of it to an auditor. That is why the most durable place for a cache in 2026 is inside a governed gateway, where caching, routing, and policy are enforced together rather than layered on afterward. Bolting a cache onto an ungoverned stack tends to create blind spots exactly where regulators and finance teams look first. The solutions below all offer caching, but they differ sharply in how much governance comes with it, and in how much of that governance you have to assemble yourself.

What to look for in a governed caching gateway

Begin with the policy model, not the cache. Can you cap spend and enforce it in the request path per team, app, and user, or do budgets only surface after the money is gone? Can cache behavior be scoped per route, with freshness and similarity thresholds under your control? Then check the safety path: a cache should respect the same guardrails for PII, secrets, and prompt injection as a live call. Finally, demand unified observability, so cost, latency, and hit rates are visible by model, team, and user, and every decision leaves an audit trail.

The best governed caching solutions in 2026

TrueFoundry

TrueFoundry starts from governance and treats caching as something enforced within it. Response and semantic caching run at the gateway, but they sit beside per-team, per-app, and per-user budgets that are applied in the request path, not reconciled after spend. The same layer carries guardrails with no external credentials, including PII and PHI detection, secrets detection, content moderation, and prompt-injection checks, plus Cedar and OPA policies, so a cached response is subject to the same rules as a fresh one. Observability is unified across cost, latency, and errors by model, team, and user, with full request tracing and no instrumentation required. Architecturally, a central control plane holds routing and config while a regional gateway plane sits alone in the request path, which is how TrueFoundry keeps caching, routing, and policy coherent as a single system. You can see how it fits together on the LLM gateway page. The trade-off is that it is a platform commitment, heavier than teams wanting only a cache library will want.

Portkey

Portkey is a well-rounded gateway that bundles caching with routing and guardrails, and it is a reasonable fit for teams wanting governance without building their own proxy. Its caching is dependable and the gateway abstractions are clean. The limitation is operational: reaching a tightly enforced, multi-team policy posture still takes meaningful configuration and ongoing management on your side, so governance is available but not quite turnkey at scale.

Martian

Martian focuses on intelligent model routing, directing each request to the model best suited to it, which can meaningfully lower cost and improve quality. For teams whose primary pain is picking the right model per request, that specialization is valuable. The limitation is breadth: it is a router first, so comprehensive caching, budget enforcement, and the full guardrail and audit surface of a governance platform are not its center of gravity, and you may need to supplement it.

LiteLLM

LiteLLM is a popular open-source proxy that unifies many providers behind one API and includes caching and basic budget features. It is flexible, transparent, and free to run, which makes it a favorite for teams that want control and are comfortable operating infrastructure. That ownership is the cost: production-grade governance, deep observability, advanced guardrails, and high-availability operations are yours to build and maintain, which is real engineering effort over time.

The verdict

If you only need caching, a library will do. But the question this list answers is what to use when caching has to coexist with routing and governance, and there the answers diverge. Martian is the specialist router, LiteLLM the flexible open-source proxy you operate yourself, and Portkey the capable gateway that still asks you to configure policy carefully. TrueFoundry is the strongest fit when governance is the starting point rather than an afterthought, because budgets, guardrails, observability, and semantic caching are enforced together in one control plane. For regulated or multi-team environments, that integration is what keeps a cache from quietly becoming a compliance gap.
